Beyond Open-Source Adoption: Open-Source Developer Activity and Platform Ecosystem Market Performance

Chengzi (Charles) Zhu

Instrumental variables and dynamic panel estimates

Research overviewIV estimatesSystem GMM estimates

Research Overview

Platforms can benefit from adopting open-source strategies. Yet openness itself merely creates the opportunity for external developers to participate. For platforms that have already adopted open-source production, whether greater developer participation and technical contribution translate into superior market performance is an open question. I examine this question using a unique large-scale longitudinal dataset of blockchain platforms, comprising 112 platform ecosystems, 9,248 platform ecosystem-month observations, 436,470 developers, and 15,130,471 development records. I find, unexpectedly, little robust evidence that greater open-source developer activity improves platform market performance. However, I find that greater developer activity prior to token listing is positively associated with platforms’ initial market valuations. Together, these findings suggest that the informational value of observable technical activity may depend on the stage of market development. Open-source developer activity may function as a signal to capital market participants when market-based information is scarce but provide less incremental information once public markets generate alternative signals about platform quality and value. (Further tests are needed to substantiate this interpretation.)

Scope of this page: The tables below report post-listing IV and System GMM estimates for their respective regression samples. Pre-listing valuation results are not displayed here. Dataset totals above refer to the full research dataset.
